Case 01 · IT general controls · ISACA teaching case

Tampa Bay Office Furniture

A fictional office-furniture manufacturer with an AS/400 accounting system. The audit team reviews system access, program changes, and computer operations.

Material in the case

  • 25 numbered audit notes drawn from staff interviews, auditor observations, system documentation, and logs.
  • Context about the company and IT environment.
  • An exercise asking readers to assess controls and propose tests.

Source

ISACA, Tampa Bay Office Furniture Inc.: A Case Study (2011).

Open public case transcription ↗

The accessible link is a third-party transcription of the ISACA publication. Attribute ISACA when using the case.

The notes are already assembled as a teaching case; individual underlying logs and interview transcripts are not supplied separately.

Case 02 · Financial / operational controls · OIG audit

Peace Corps Purchase Card Program

An audit of policies, cardholder oversight, purchase approval, monitoring, and risk assessment in the Peace Corps purchase-card program.

Material in the report

  • Audit scope and methods, including document and transaction analysis, interviews, and direct observations.
  • Reported transaction exceptions and control observations.
  • Findings, recommendations, and management responses.

Source

Peace Corps Office of Inspector General, Final Audit Report: Peace Corps’ Purchase Card Program (2009).

Open official report PDF ↗

48-page report hosted by Oversight.gov.

Interviews and transaction evidence are reported in the audit; the original interview transcripts and complete transaction packets are not included.

Case 03 · Contractor payments / IT assets · GAO audit

FBI Trilogy Project

An audit of contractor invoice review, questioned project costs, equipment purchasing, and accountability for assets acquired during a major FBI technology program.

Material in the report

  • Audit objectives and methods, including invoice review, interviews, walkthroughs, data analysis, and physical asset work.
  • Examples of questionable costs and missing-asset results.
  • GAO conclusions, recommendations, and agency comments.

Source

U.S. Government Accountability Office, Weak Controls over Trilogy Project Led to Payment of Questionable Contractor Costs and Missing Assets, GAO-06-306 (2006).
