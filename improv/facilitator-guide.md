# Facilitator Guide: Audit Interview Improv

## Purpose

Give students a brief, low-stakes rehearsal of the activity they will later design for an AI stakeholder: open an interview, understand the process, ask follow-up questions, request evidence, evaluate the answers, and summarize next steps.

## Setup

1. Form pairs. Assign one person as auditor and one as auditee.
2. Give the auditor the [auditor brief](auditor-brief.md) and [transaction list](artifacts/pay-buddy-transaction-list.md).
3. Assign a scenario number and personality letter (A–D), then give the auditee that matching role card from [role-cards](role-cards/). For example, “Scenario 2, Personality C.” Keep the card hidden from the auditor.
4. Keep the related scenario evidence with the facilitator. Auditees may share it only when the role card's disclosure cue is met and the auditor requests it.

## Suggested 12-minute activity

- 1 minute: silently read roles and prepare.
- 5 minutes: conduct the interview.
- 3 minutes: compare the auditor's working theory with what was established by evidence.
- 3 minutes: debrief, then switch roles or distribute a second scenario.

## Facilitator ground rules

- The auditor should not treat a communication style, incomplete training, or a suspicious-looking record as proof of a policy violation.
- The recurring $29.95 Pay Buddy display is a lead. The actual merchant, purpose, authorization, and circumstances must be established.
- Scenario 1 may have a legitimate work purpose and verbal managerial authorization; neither fact alone settles card-policy compliance. The employee's recurring training is incomplete, but that does not prove misuse.
- Scenario 2 involves a spouse's online purchase using the wrong similar-looking card. The auditor should clarify what happened and corroborate which card was used. The email describes an introductory rate of $29.95 through December 2026, followed by a scheduled $295.00 monthly rate beginning January 2027. The April–September records are consistent with the introductory rate; the future price change is not itself evidence of a current discrepancy or policy violation.
- Scenario 3 includes deliberate personal use and a false business explanation. The suspicious email is a lead to corroborate, not proof by itself. This is one scenario, not an inference to apply to the others.
- Do not invent policy clauses or claim the fictional organization has reached a disciplinary conclusion. For the role-play, participants know that travel-card policy violations can have serious consequences.
- Treat all records as fictional classroom props. Do not enter real employee, payment-card, or company data into an AI platform.

## Debrief questions

1. Which opening question helped establish the process and purpose?
2. What did the auditor learn from the transaction list, and what remained unknown?
3. Which follow-up question connected a claim to evidence?
4. What alternative explanation should be considered before concluding there is a violation?
5. What further evidence, corroboration, or next interview would be appropriate?
6. How should the interviewer summarize facts separately from assumptions?
